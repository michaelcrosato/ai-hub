About this little universe.

Three pilots. One stolen sunrise. An original arcade flight adventure inspired by the momentum, squadron camaraderie and branching spectacle of StarFox 64.

Original universe, characters, spacecraft, terrain, interface, music and effects. All game art is built from native Three.js geometry and materials. No Nintendo assets, recordings, characters or dialogue are included.

Engine Feedback

Liked most: Three.js geometry and scene composition made it straightforward to build a coherent original low-poly world.

Stuck or guessed wrong: The required strict WebGPU mode needed source inspection: r180 installs an automatic WebGL fallback, which this build explicitly disables. Simulation tests also caught a missing delta-time multiplier in enemy motion.

Most important custom system: The complete flight-combat simulation, mission timeline, branching route, wingmate rescue system, boss phases, controller menus and attract pilot.

Hardest part: Coordinating world-space flight, targeting, collision queries and a camera-relative HUD across rail and all-range modes without a rendered playthrough.

Remove or overhaul: The implicit renderer fallback policy; backend requirements should be explicit application configuration.

One engine change: A stable public WebGPU-only option with clear capability and device-loss reporting.

Prompt Feedback

Prompt version: 404299fcd4

Liked most: The complete vertical-slice requirement and controller-first attract mode made the desired experience concrete.

Confusing: Native engine art style and assets is ambiguous for Three.js, which provides rendering primitives rather than a house art style or game asset library. I interpreted it as original native procedural geometry.

Hardest: Building coherent mission pacing, responsive flight and polished menus while honoring the prohibition on visual testing.

One additional test: A full real-controller playthrough in a native WebGPU browser, covering both axis toggles, the branch, rescues, checkpoints and the final boss.
