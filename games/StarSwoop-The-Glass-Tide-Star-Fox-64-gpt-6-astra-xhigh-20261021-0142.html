StarSwoop — The Glass Tide
An original flight-combat vertical slice inspired by StarFox 64. All characters, ships, world geometry, music, and sound effects were created for this demo. This is an independent work, not affiliated with Nintendo.

Engine feedback

What worked best? Three.js gives direct control over a complete 3D scene while remaining easy to ship inside a single offline HTML file.

Where did you get stuck or guess wrong? Native Enter behavior duplicated the custom menu action. Preventing the browser default fixed it. The verification harness also needed timed polling because it was driving animation frames manually. No engine blocker remained.

Most important custom system? The combat and mission layer: steering, aim assistance, swept projectile collisions, branching objectives, squad support, checkpoint snapshots, and a controller-driven UI.

Hardest part? Coordinating camera-relative aiming, moving formations, boss weak points, and readable targeting feedback across one continuous flight.

What would you remove or overhaul? The manual resource-lifecycle bookkeeping required for short-lived meshes and materials.

One change? A standard gameplay layer for input actions, collision queries, and deterministic mission sequencing would make the renderer a more complete game toolkit.

Prompt feedback · 404299fcd4

What worked best? The explicit controller-first philosophy, single-file delivery requirement, and attract-mode behavior gave the demo a clear product shape.

What was confusing? “Feature-complete” is open-ended for a spiritual successor. This slice defines its boundary as one complete branching mission with progression, squad support, a boss, checkpoints, and a debrief.

Hardest thing? Making combat, environmental progression, menu navigation, and demonstration playback feel like one coherent game without visual review.

One additional test? A full real DualSense playthrough on a television to evaluate flight feel, control latency, legibility, and default axis direction.

Design scope

One roughly five-minute mission, two mutually exclusive routes, three weapon levels, three boss weak points, and three difficulty settings. No touch controls. Settings and personal-best scores persist locally when browser storage is available.
