What worked best?

Three.js makes a coherent procedural world possible with a compact geometry and material vocabulary. Rapier supplies explicit collision events.

Where did we get stuck or guess wrong?

Three.js r180 installs a WebGL fallback by default. The build explicitly disables that callback before initialization to enforce WebGPU-only rendering. Initial headless Chrome captures were blank; headed Chrome under Xvfb rendered the scene correctly.

The most important custom system?

The mission director: combat pacing, squad dialogue, route choices, checkpoints, boss choreography and the autonomous attract pilot.

The hardest part of the engine?

Coordinating camera motion, aiming and collision feedback so high-speed flight remains readable.

What would we overhaul?

The amount of application infrastructure needed around rendering: input navigation, sound scheduling, game-state transitions and encounter authoring.

One change?

A first-class, portable WebGPU capability report with actionable initialization diagnostics.

PROMPT VERSION 404299fcd4

What worked best?

The controller-first requirement and autonomous attract mode give the demo a clear arcade identity.

What was confusing?

“Feature-complete” and “all infrastructure” have open-ended scope for one vertical slice. The demo treats one complete, replayable sortie as its boundary.

What was hardest?

Bringing flight feel, encounter variety, branching progression and audiovisual presentation together in a single portable HTML file.

If there were one additional test?

Play the full sortie and every menu with a physical DualSense controller on a WebGPU-capable browser, including reconnecting it mid-flight.
