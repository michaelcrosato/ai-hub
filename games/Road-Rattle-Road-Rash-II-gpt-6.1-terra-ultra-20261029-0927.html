The rules of the road

Ride dirty. Finish first.

Keep it upright

Hold R2 to accelerate, L2 to brake, and steer with the left stick. Stay on the asphalt: dirt slows you down and traffic hurts.

Make some room

Tap □ to swing left or ○ to swing right when a rival is alongside. Watch the hit flash and knock them off their line.

Burn rubber

Hold ✕ for a speed boost. Your boost meter refills as you ride. Take risks to pass the pack before the finish.

Get back up

Your bike takes damage from crashes and fights. Wipe out and you'll lose time while your crew gets you rolling again.

Keyboard: arrows or WASD • space boost • Z / X strike • Esc pause

Source Information

Date: 2026-10-29 09:27

Source: Road Rash II

Tech: Three.js r180 WebGPU renderer; Rapier 0.19.3 physics; Web Audio API; Gamepad API; PeerJS 1.5.5; AI Hub session protocol 1.3.0; HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

Art Direction: Original stylized cel-shaded low-poly coastal highway, riders and traffic.

Animation: Procedural transforms and simulation-driven bike motion.

Audio: Original code-synthesized surf-punk music, engine, sound effects and gibberish rider voices.

Online: AI Hub race sessions and standalone private room-code multiplayer via PeerJS.

Testing: Lightweight syntax and headless Chrome interaction checks; Headless software WebGPU produced a blank canvas even for a minimal independent Three.js scene; Full race finish and multiplayer race synchronization remain unverified; two-player standalone room signaling passed. Performance targets and optimization are out of scope.

Environment: Codex CLI (headless); Linux; browser Google Chrome 154.

Model: OpenAI, gpt-6.1-terra, reasoning effort ultra.

Usage: API cost unknown; token count unknown; session time unknown.

Testing Feedback

Issues caught: 2 confirmed and fixed. Critical / game breaking: 1 (bundled scripts were initially commented out); the other was a HUD overlap.

Most helpful test: browser startup and race interaction. Least helpful test: syntax check alone, because it cannot exercise WebGPU or gameplay.

Engine Feedback

Liked most: Three.js made a procedural scene and lighting quick to assemble.

Stuck or guessed wrong: WebGPU availability varies by browser and graphics environment; adapter detection was necessary.

Most important thing built: race logic, rider behavior, collisions and controller-first interface.

Hardest part: integrating the asynchronous WebGPU renderer with physics, gameplay and networking in one file.

Remove or overhaul: simplify WebGPU initialization diagnostics.

One change: provide a clearer, explicit WebGPU-only initialization option.

Prompt Feedback

Liked most: the clear creative direction and controller-first focus.

Confusing: the precise scope of multiplayer parity between standalone rooms and hub sessions was open to interpretation.

Hardest: fitting racing, combat, networking, procedural audiovisuals and a polished interface into one portable file.

One test to run: a full four-player controller race on separate real devices under varying network conditions.
