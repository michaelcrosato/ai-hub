What worked best?
Three.js makes custom meshes, materials, lights, and a readable scene graph easy to combine into an original flight world.
Where did we get stuck or guess wrong?
The package manager’s default cache was read-only. Moving its cache into the project resolved installation. No visual outcome was verified.
What had to be built?
The flight simulation, mission scripting, collision detection, targeting, enemy AI, controller UX, checkpoints, sound synthesis, and attract autopilot.
What was hardest?
Keeping targeting, camera projection, and world-space motion consistent across the simulation and presentation.
What would we overhaul?
The manual lifetime management of transient geometry and materials.
One engine change?
A first-party gameplay layer with deterministic simulation and controller navigation would make complete vertical slices easier.

What worked best?
The controller-first requirement and explicit attract mode shaped a coherent arcade experience. A single-file deliverable gave the project a clear finish line.
What was confusing?
“Native art style and assets” is ambiguous for a rendering library with no supplied game assets. This demo uses original native mesh geometry.
What was hardest?
Delivering a full mission, broad mechanics, and polished presentation while honoring the prohibition on visual testing.
One test we would run?
A full mission with a physical PS5 controller, checking stick direction, menu focus, targeting, checkpoint retry, and the boss encounter.
