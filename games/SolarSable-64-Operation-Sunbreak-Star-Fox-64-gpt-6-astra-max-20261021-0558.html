What worked best?

Three.js made procedural spacecraft, atmospheric lighting, camera motion, and bloom easy to combine in one portable scene.

Where did you get stuck or guess wrong?

Text checks caught duplicate Enter activation from native button behavior; this was fixed. Code review also corrected projectile cleanup during boss defeat. No Three.js API blocker was encountered.

Most important custom system?

The data-driven mission director and shared combat simulation: branching objectives, checkpoints, target locks, and both rail and all-range flight.

Hardest part?

Keeping aiming, collisions, target indicators, and camera framing consistent as flight changes from a scrolling corridor to a 360-degree arena.

What would you overhaul?

The amount of manual resource and effect lifetime management required around a scene graph.

One engine change?

A small integrated gameplay layer for fixed-step simulation, input actions, and reusable entity lifecycles, without imposing an editor.

What worked best?

The explicit vertical-slice scope, controller-first design, and idle attract mode gave the game a clear identity and a complete player journey.

What was confusing?

“Unlimited performance” cannot remove browser or hardware limits. The demo prioritizes scene detail, with no performance benchmark or optimization target.

Hardest requirement?

Delivering a coherent, finished mission, original art, responsive controls, and readable interfaces while intentionally doing no visual testing.

One additional test?

A full mission played with a physical DualSense controller on a television, including route selection, reconnecting the controller, and both axis inversion toggles.
