What worked best? Three.js scene composition and TSL bloom made the visual language straightforward. Rapier provides a separate, explicit collision world.

Stuck or wrong guesses? r180 enables automatic WebGL fallback. Inspecting the pinned source identified its hook; this demo disables it. Runtime behavior is unconfirmed.

Most important custom system? The mission director, shared controller and keyboard input, and the combat state machine.

Hardest part? Keeping chase-camera aiming, the rail-to-arena transition, and collision ownership consistent.

What would I overhaul? Implicit graphics-backend fallback.
One change? A documented strict-WebGPU option with first-class initialization errors.

What worked best? The exact stack, controller-first interface, kiosk attract mode, and complete vertical-slice goal gave the game a clear shape.

What was confusing? “All infrastructure” is unbounded. Here it means an extensible, data-directed mission with complete gameplay, menus, saves, checkpoints, audio, and results.

What was hardest? Giving a branching canyon run, an all-range boss, accessible controls, and procedural sound enough room in a single-file deliverable.

One test I would run? A complete DualSense playthrough covering axis inversion, both routes, checkpoint retries, disconnect recovery, attract-mode exit, and both boss phases in a WebGPU browser.
